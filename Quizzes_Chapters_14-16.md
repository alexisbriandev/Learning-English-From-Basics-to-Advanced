## Chapter 14 Quiz: Punctuation and Capitalization

### Questions

1. Which sentence uses end punctuation correctly?  
   A. The workshop begins at noon?  
   B. When does the workshop begin.  
   C. The workshop begins at noon.  
   D. Please arrive by noon?

2. Which sentence is punctuated as a direct question?  
   A. I wonder when the office opens?  
   B. When does the office open?  
   C. The office opens at nine?  
   D. Please tell me when the office opens?

3. Which sentence uses the comma in a series correctly?  
   A. We need pens notebooks, folders, and labels.  
   B. We need pens, notebooks, folders, and labels.  
   C. We need, pens, notebooks folders and labels.  
   D. We need pens notebooks folders and, labels.

4. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence with an introductory phrase.  
   A. After the meeting we emailed the summary.  
   B. After the meeting, we emailed the summary.  
   C. After, the meeting we emailed the summary.  
   D. After the meeting; we emailed the summary.

5. Which sentence correctly joins two independent clauses with a coordinating conjunction?  
   A. The room was ready, and the guests arrived.  
   B. The room, was ready and the guests arrived.  
   C. The room was ready and, the guests arrived.  
   D. The room was ready, the guests arrived.

6. Which sentence correctly sets off nonessential information?  
   A. Dr. Chen who teaches biology is leading the tour.  
   B. Dr. Chen, who teaches biology, is leading the tour.  
   C. Dr. Chen who teaches biology, is leading the tour.  
   D. Dr. Chen, who teaches biology is leading the tour.

7. Which sentence avoids an incorrect comma between a subject and its verb?  
   A. The list of required materials, is attached.  
   B. The list, of required materials is attached.  
   C. The list of required materials is attached.  
   D. The list of required, materials is attached.

8. Which sentence correctly uses a semicolon to join closely related independent clauses?  
   A. The survey is closed; the team is analyzing the responses.  
   B. The survey; is closed, the team is analyzing the responses.  
   C. The survey is closed,; the team is analyzing the responses.  
   D. The survey is closed; because the team is analyzing the responses.

9. Which sentence correctly uses a colon to introduce a list?  
   A. Bring: your ID, the form, and a pen.  
   B. Bring three items: your ID, the form, and a pen.  
   C. Bring three items your ID: the form, and a pen.  
   D. Bring three items; your ID, the form, and a pen.

10. Which sentence uses an apostrophe in a contraction correctly?  
    A. Its ready to send.  
    B. It's ready to send.  
    C. Its' ready to send.  
    D. It,s ready to send.

11. Which sentence uses a possessive apostrophe correctly for one employee?  
    A. The employees badge is on the desk.  
    B. The employee's badge is on the desk.  
    C. The employees' badge is on the desk.  
    D. The employee badge's is on the desk.

12. Which sentence uses a possessive apostrophe correctly for multiple students?  
    A. The student's projects were displayed.  
    B. The students projects' were displayed.  
    C. The students' projects were displayed.  
    D. The students's projects were displayed.

13. Which sentence uses an apostrophe correctly with an ordinary plural?  
    A. Three laptop's are available.  
    B. Three laptops are available.  
    C. Three laptops' are available.  
    D. Three laptop's are available's.

14. Which sentence punctuates direct speech correctly?  
    A. “The file is ready.” Priya said.  
    B. “The file is ready,” Priya said.  
    C. “The file is ready”, Priya said.  
    D. “The file is ready” Priya, said.

15. Which sentence correctly introduces a quotation with attribution?  
    A. The guide states, “Save a copy before editing.”  
    B. The guide states “Save a copy before editing”.  
    C. The guide states; “Save a copy before editing.”  
    D. The guide states: “Save a copy before editing”.

16. Which sentence uses parentheses for supplementary information?  
    A. The training (which is optional) begins at 10 a.m.  
    B. The training which (is optional begins at 10 a.m.  
    C. The training which is optional) begins at 10 a.m.  
    D. The training (which is optional begins at 10 a.m.

17. Which sentence uses a hyphen in a compound modifier before a noun?  
    A. The team prepared a well organized report.  
    B. The team prepared a well-organized report.  
    C. The team prepared a well—organized report.  
    D. The team prepared a well; organized report.

18. Which sentence uses a dash to mark an emphatic break?  
    A. One issue remained—the missing signature.  
    B. One issue remained, the missing signature.  
    C. One issue remained; the missing signature,  
    D. One issue remained:—the missing signature.

19. Which sentence is capitalized correctly?  
    A. My sister studies english on monday.  
    B. My sister studies English on Monday.  
    C. My Sister studies English on monday.  
    D. My sister studies English On Monday.

20. Which sentence capitalizes a proper place name correctly?  
    A. We traveled to lake victoria in july.  
    B. We traveled to Lake Victoria in July.  
    C. We traveled to Lake victoria in July.  
    D. We traveled to lake Victoria in july.

21. Which heading uses sentence-style capitalization?  
    A. How To Prepare For A Meeting  
    B. How to prepare for a meeting  
    C. HOW TO PREPARE FOR A MEETING  
    D. how to prepare for a meeting

22. Which sentence uses a comma correctly after an introductory dependent clause?  
    A. Because the train was delayed we arrived late.  
    B. Because the train was delayed, we arrived late.  
    C. Because, the train was delayed we arrived late.  
    D. Because the train, was delayed, we arrived late.

23. Which sentence correctly punctuates a direct quotation followed by an attribution in standard American edited style?  
    A. “We will send the update tomorrow”, the coordinator said.  
    B. “We will send the update tomorrow,” the coordinator said.  
    C. “We will send the update tomorrow” the coordinator said.  
    D. “We will send the update tomorrow;” the coordinator said.

24. Which revision correctly punctuates a list introduced by a complete clause?  
    A. The application requires: a résumé, two references, and a writing sample.  
    B. The application requires three items: a résumé, two references, and a writing sample.  
    C. The application requires three items, a résumé: two references, and a writing sample.  
    D. The application: requires three items a résumé, two references, and a writing sample.

25. Which statement about punctuation is accurate?  
    A. Punctuation only records pauses in speech.  
    B. Punctuation helps readers understand sentence structure and meaning.  
    C. A comma can always join two complete sentences.  
    D. Every sentence with a quotation needs a colon.

### Answer key

1. **C.** A statement ends with a period.  
2. **B.** It is a direct question, so it ends with a question mark.  
3. **B.** Commas separate the items in the series; this book follows the serial comma convention.  
4. **B.** A comma commonly follows an introductory phrase.  
5. **A.** A comma plus *and* joins two independent clauses.  
6. **B.** The relative clause adds nonessential information and is enclosed by commas.  
7. **C.** Do not separate a subject from its verb with a comma.  
8. **A.** Both sides are independent clauses, and the semicolon joins them.  
9. **B.** A colon follows a complete introductory clause and introduces the list.  
10. **B.** *It's* means *it is*.  
11. **B.** A singular noun generally forms the possessive with apostrophe + *s*.  
12. **C.** For a regular plural ending in *s*, put the apostrophe after the *s*.  
13. **B.** Ordinary plurals do not take apostrophes.  
14. **B.** In standard American punctuation, the comma goes inside the closing quotation mark.  
15. **A.** The sentence uses a comma to introduce a short quotation; the period is inside the closing quotation mark.  
16. **A.** The parentheses enclose the supplementary phrase and are balanced.  
17. **B.** The hyphen joins the compound modifier *well-organized* before the noun.  
18. **A.** The dash marks a break before an emphasized explanation.  
19. **B.** Languages and days of the week are capitalized; *sister* is not a title or proper name here.  
20. **B.** The proper name *Lake Victoria* and the month *July* are capitalized.  
21. **B.** Sentence-style capitalization capitalizes the first word and proper nouns, not every major word.  
22. **B.** A comma follows an introductory dependent clause.  
23. **B.** In standard American style, the comma goes inside the closing quotation mark.  
24. **B.** The words before the colon form a complete clause that introduces the list.  
25. **B.** Punctuation guides readers through structure and meaning, not just spoken pauses.

## Chapter 15 Quiz: Grammar, Usage, Rules, and Common Mistakes

### Questions

1. Which description best defines grammar?  
   A. A writer's preferred tone and level of detail  
   B. Patterns that shape words, phrases, and sentences  
   C. A list of spelling choices only  
   D. The vocabulary used by one profession

2. Which description best defines usage?  
   A. Choices accepted or preferred in a particular community or context  
   B. The punctuation system of one book  
   C. The order of paragraphs in an essay  
   D. The meaning of a single word only

3. Which description best defines style?  
   A. Sentence patterns only  
   B. Choices made for clarity, tone, purpose, and consistency  
   C. Regional pronunciation  
   D. A list of irregular verbs

4. What is the main focus of this book's editing guidance?  
   A. One variety of spoken English as the only valid variety  
   B. Standard edited English for academic and professional settings  
   C. Informal slang in every setting  
   D. A rule that every English speaker must use identical forms

5. Choose the correct word: ____ going to present their project today.  
   A. Their  
   B. There  
   C. They're  
   D. Theirs

6. Choose the correct word: The organization changed ____ name last year.  
   A. it's  
   B. its  
   C. its'  
   D. it

7. Choose the correct word: ____ welcome to use the meeting room.  
   A. Your  
   B. You're  
   C. Yours  
   D. Youre

8. Choose the correct word: The second draft is clearer ____ the first.  
   A. then  
   B. than  
   C. there  
   D. that

9. Choose the best word for standard edited writing: We received ____ applications this month than last month.  
   A. less  
   B. fewer  
   C. little  
   D. much

10. Choose the correct pronoun in this formal sentence: To ____ should I send the signed agreement?  
    A. who  
    B. whom  
    C. whoever  
    D. whose

11. Choose the correct verb: The set of instructions ____ included with the device.  
    A. are  
    B. were  
    C. is  
    D. have

12. Which sentence uses parallel structure?  
    A. The position includes scheduling meetings, answering questions, and preparing reports.  
    B. The position includes scheduling meetings, to answer questions, and reports are prepared.  
    C. The position includes to schedule meetings, answering questions, and prepare reports.  
    D. The position includes meetings, answering questions, and to prepare reports.

13. Which sentence is the standard edited form when the intended meaning is a single negative?  
    A. I didn't find no errors.  
    B. I didn't find any errors.  
    C. I didn't find none errors.  
    D. I found not no errors.

14. Choose the correct word: The new schedule may ____ the delivery time.  
    A. effect  
    B. affect  
    C. effects  
    D. affected (as a present-tense verb)

15. Choose the correct word: The policy will ____ all new applicants.  
    A. except  
    B. accept  
    C. acceptance  
    D. excepted

16. Choose the correct word: Thank you for your useful ____.  
    A. advise  
    B. advice  
    C. advised  
    D. advising

17. Choose the correct word: The blue curtains ____ the room's warm colors.  
    A. compliment  
    B. complement  
    C. compliments  
    D. complemented (as a present-tense verb)

18. Choose the correct verb: Please ____ the form on the table before the meeting.  
    A. lie  
    B. lay  
    C. lain  
    D. laid (as a base-form command)

19. Which sentence has consistent past tense?  
    A. The researcher collected the data and analyzes the results.  
    B. The researcher collects the data and analyzed the results.  
    C. The researcher collected the data and analyzed the results.  
    D. The researcher had collected the data and analyzes the results.

20. Which sentence has a clear pronoun reference?  
    A. When the manager spoke to the assistant, she approved the request.  
    B. The laptop was beside the tablet, and it needed charging.  
    C. The editor returned the draft to Sam, who revised it that afternoon.  
    D. They said it was ready, but no one knew what they meant.

21. Which sentence uses a preposition correctly in standard usage?  
    A. We discussed about the deadline.  
    B. We discussed the deadline.  
    C. We discussed on the deadline.  
    D. We discussed regarding about the deadline.

22. Which sentence has a correctly placed modifier?  
    A. After reviewing the proposal, the manager approved it.  
    B. After reviewing the proposal, it was approved by the manager.  
    C. Covered in notes, the student read the report.  
    D. The manager approved the proposal after reviewing it, which was helpful.

23. Which sentence is a comma splice?  
    A. The meeting ended, and the team returned to work.  
    B. The meeting ended; the team returned to work.  
    C. The meeting ended, the team returned to work.  
    D. After the meeting ended, the team returned to work.

24. Which editing sequence best follows the book's repeatable routine?  
    A. Check commas, format headings, then decide what the message means.  
    B. Read for meaning, check sentence structure, review word forms and agreement, then edit mechanics and reread.  
    C. Run a grammar checker, accept every change, and submit immediately.  
    D. Correct spelling only, because structure is a matter of preference.

25. A grammar checker suggests changing a sentence, but the change seems to alter the writer's meaning. What is the best next step?  
    A. Accept it automatically because software is always correct.  
    B. Delete the whole sentence.  
    C. Check the exact issue in a reliable reference, compare examples, and keep a revision only if it preserves meaning and fits the context.  
    D. Ignore all grammar checkers and references.

### Answer key

1. **B.** Grammar concerns the patterns that shape phrases and sentences.  
2. **A.** Usage concerns forms accepted or preferred in a community, situation, or variety.  
3. **B.** Style choices serve clarity, tone, purpose, and consistency.  
4. **B.** The book focuses on standard edited English in academic and professional writing while recognizing valid varieties.  
5. **C — They're.** It means *they are*.  
6. **B — its.** *Its* is possessive; *it's* means *it is* or *it has*.  
7. **B — You're.** It means *you are*.  
8. **B — than.** *Than* introduces a comparison.  
9. **B — fewer.** *Applications* is a plural count noun.  
10. **B — whom.** It is the object of the preposition *to* in the formal construction.  
11. **C — is.** The subject is singular *set*, not *instructions*.  
12. **A.** The three items use parallel *-ing* forms.  
13. **B.** In standard edited writing, use one negative marker for this intended negative meaning.  
14. **B — affect.** It is the verb meaning “influence.”  
15. **B — accept.** *Accept* means to receive or approve; *except* means not including.  
16. **B — advice.** *Advice* is the noun; *advise* is the verb.  
17. **B — complement.** It means to complete or go well with something.  
18. **B — lay.** *Lay* means to put something down and takes an object, here *the form*.  
19. **C.** Both main verbs are simple past forms.  
20. **C.** *Who* clearly refers to Sam, and *it* refers to the draft.  
21. **B.** In this meaning, *discuss* takes a direct object without *about*.  
22. **A.** The manager is the person who reviewed the proposal and approved it.  
23. **C.** It joins two independent clauses with only a comma.  
24. **B.** It follows the sequence: meaning, structure, forms and agreement, mechanics, and a final reread.  
25. **C.** A checker can miss context; investigate the issue and preserve the intended meaning.

## Chapter 16 Quiz: The Paragraph

### Questions

1. What is a paragraph?  
   A. A fixed number of sentences separated by blank lines  
   B. A group of related sentences that develops one main point or purpose  
   C. A list of unrelated facts  
   D. A sentence that always has a topic sentence and conclusion

2. What is the main job of a topic sentence?  
   A. To state or signal the paragraph's main point  
   B. To provide every example in the paragraph  
   C. To introduce an unrelated topic  
   D. To correct punctuation in the paragraph

3. Which sentence is the strongest topic sentence for a paragraph about preparing for a job interview?  
   A. Interviews can take place on different days.  
   B. Preparing in advance helps applicants answer questions with greater confidence.  
   C. My friend bought a blue notebook last year.  
   D. The office has two elevators.

4. Which sentence best supports the topic sentence *Planning meals at home can reduce weekday stress*?  
   A. A weekly meal plan can save time deciding what to cook after work.  
   B. My neighbor prefers a different color for the kitchen.  
   C. Some restaurants have outdoor seating.  
   D. The train station is near the market.

5. Which sentence weakens unity in a paragraph about the benefits of walking to work?  
   A. Walking can add physical activity to a daily routine.  
   B. The route may include a park or other pleasant scenery.  
   C. Several colleagues share a spreadsheet for tracking expenses.  
   D. Walking may also reduce the need to find parking.

6. What does coherence mean in a paragraph?  
   A. Every sentence has the same length.  
   B. Ideas connect clearly and follow a sensible order.  
   C. Every sentence begins with a transition.  
   D. The paragraph includes as many details as possible.

7. What does development mean in a paragraph?  
   A. Giving the main point enough explanation and support for the reader's purpose  
   B. Adding a new topic in every sentence  
   C. Using a concluding sentence in every paragraph  
   D. Making every sentence longer

8. Which pattern is most useful for explaining how to submit an online application?  
   A. Comparison  
   B. Sequence  
   C. Problem and solution  
   D. Cause and effect

9. Which pattern best organizes a paragraph that explains two ways a morning and evening class differ?  
   A. Comparison  
   B. Sequence  
   C. Problem and solution  
   D. Example

10. Which pattern best fits a paragraph that describes a long wait for service and proposes a new booking system?  
    A. Sequence  
    B. Problem and solution  
    C. Comparison  
    D. Example

11. Which pattern is used in this paragraph plan: claim, a specific instance, and explanation of how the instance supports the claim?  
    A. Example or illustration  
    B. Sequence  
    C. Comparison  
    D. Problem and solution

12. Choose the transition that best signals contrast: The first design is inexpensive. ____, it requires more maintenance.  
    A. For example  
    B. However  
    C. Therefore  
    D. First

13. Choose the transition that best signals a result: The supplier missed the delivery date. ____, the installation was postponed.  
    A. As a result  
    B. In contrast  
    C. For example  
    D. Meanwhile

14. Choose the transition that best introduces an example: Several tools can help writers revise. ____, reading a draft aloud can reveal missing words.  
    A. Therefore  
    B. However  
    C. For example  
    D. In contrast

15. Why should transitions be chosen based on logic?  
    A. A transition should accurately signal the relationship between ideas.  
    B. Every paragraph requires at least four transitions.  
    C. Transitions make supporting details unnecessary.  
    D. Any transition can be used to make writing sound formal.

16. When is a new paragraph most clearly needed?  
    A. When the writer moves to a new main point or stage of the discussion  
    B. After every sentence  
    C. Whenever a sentence contains a comma  
    D. When the writer wants to avoid revising

17. Which statement about paragraph length is accurate?  
    A. Every paragraph should contain exactly five sentences.  
    B. Paragraph length depends on purpose, complexity, and reader needs.  
    C. A paragraph should never contain more than three sentences.  
    D. Longer paragraphs are always more developed.

18. Which detail should be removed from a paragraph focused on an upcoming software training session?  
    A. The date and time of the training  
    B. What participants should bring  
    C. The skills participants will practice  
    D. The cafeteria's new weekend menu

19. Which order best follows a paragraph-writing process?  
    A. Edit punctuation, choose a reader, gather ideas, decide the purpose  
    B. Identify purpose and reader, gather and group ideas, draft a controlling point, arrange support, write, revise, edit  
    C. Write random sentences, add transitions, then choose a topic  
    D. Choose a conclusion, remove all examples, then check the title

20. Which revision best improves unity in a paragraph about later library hours?  
    A. Add a sentence about the cafeteria's new menu.  
    B. Keep details about study access and remove an unrelated sentence about parking fees.  
    C. Replace every sentence with a transition word.  
    D. Add three unrelated campus facts.

21. Which revision best improves development in a paragraph that claims a writing center helps students but gives no explanation?  
    A. Add a specific example of how a tutor helps a student plan or revise a draft.  
    B. Repeat the claim in four different ways.  
    C. Add a sentence about the weather.  
    D. Delete the claim.

22. Which sentence best links an example back to a paragraph's point that clear instructions reduce delays?  
    A. This example shows how a complete checklist can prevent missing documents and follow-up emails.  
    B. The instructions are printed on white paper.  
    C. Delays happen in many situations.  
    D. Another department has a different logo.

23. A paragraph discusses the problem of incomplete customer forms, possible reasons, and a proposed form redesign. Which sentence would most likely conclude it effectively?  
    A. For these reasons, adding examples beside required fields could help customers submit complete forms on the first try.  
    B. The office has blue walls.  
    C. Forms are made of paper or digital files.  
    D. I once filled out a form at a clinic.

24. Which opening best suits a concise workplace update whose purpose is to tell a team what has been completed?  
    A. There are many things to say about the project, and work can be difficult.  
    B. We completed the first review of the client survey and grouped the responses by topic.  
    C. Wow, projects are interesting.  
    D. Before I explain, consider the history of surveys.

25. Which review question best checks coherence?  
    A. Does each idea follow clearly from the one before it?  
    B. Did I use the longest possible words?  
    C. Does every sentence have the same punctuation mark?  
    D. Did I include every note I collected?

### Answer key

1. **B.** A paragraph is a group of related sentences developing one main point or purpose.  
2. **A.** A topic sentence states or signals the main point.  
3. **B.** It presents a focused point about interview preparation.  
4. **A.** It directly supports the idea that planning meals can reduce weekday stress.  
5. **C.** Expense tracking is unrelated to the benefits of walking to work.  
6. **B.** Coherence helps readers follow how ideas connect and progress.  
7. **A.** Development means providing enough explanation, evidence, examples, or reasoning.  
8. **B — Sequence.** Application instructions are best presented in the order readers follow.  
9. **A — Comparison.** The paragraph explains similarities or differences between two class times.  
10. **B — Problem and solution.** It presents a difficulty and a response to it.  
11. **A — Example or illustration.** A specific instance supports the general claim.  
12. **B — However.** It signals contrast between low cost and higher maintenance.  
13. **A — As a result.** The postponed installation results from the missed delivery.  
14. **C — For example.** It introduces a specific instance of a revision tool.  
15. **A.** Transitions should name the actual logical relationship, not decorate the paragraph.  
16. **A.** A new main point or stage is a natural reason for a paragraph break.  
17. **B.** There is no fixed sentence count; length depends on purpose and reader needs.  
18. **D.** The weekend menu does not serve the training paragraph's purpose.  
19. **B.** This sequence moves from purpose and planning through drafting, revising, and editing.  
20. **B.** Removing unrelated material keeps the paragraph focused on its main point.  
21. **A.** A concrete example explains how the writing center helps.  
22. **A.** It explains how the example supports the point about reducing delays.  
23. **A.** It summarizes the proposed response and connects it to the problem.  
24. **B.** It gives the completed work directly, matching the update's purpose.  
25. **A.** Coherence depends on clear relationships and logical progression among ideas.

## Scoring and review

Count one point for each correct response in a chapter quiz. Use the result to choose what to review; it is not a proficiency label. Revisit missed questions in the corresponding chapter, then apply the pattern in a sentence, edited passage, or paragraph of your own. For style questions, consider whether the answer fits the stated reader and purpose.
