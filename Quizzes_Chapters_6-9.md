# Chapter Quizzes: Chapters 6–9

Each chapter quiz contains 20 items. Choose the best answer unless a prompt asks for a different response. Use the answer keys to review why a choice fits; for context-dependent language choices, the question provides the context needed.

## Chapter 6 Quiz: Adverbs

### Questions

1. Which word is the adverb in *The assistant carefully checked the figures*?  
   A. assistant  
   B. carefully  
   C. checked  
   D. figures

2. What does *extremely* modify in *The instructions were extremely clear*?  
   A. instructions  
   B. were  
   C. clear  
   D. the

3. What does *quite* modify in *She answered quite confidently*?  
   A. She  
   B. answered  
   C. quite  
   D. confidently

4. Which word is an adverb of frequency?  
   A. upstairs  
   B. rarely  
   C. tomorrow  
   D. carefully

5. Which word is an adverb of place?  
   A. nearby  
   B. soon  
   C. often  
   D. gently

6. Choose the adverb that best completes the sentence: The team meets ____ to review progress.  
   A. weekly  
   B. careful  
   C. quick  
   D. good

7. Choose the correct form: The new employee completed the task ____.  
   A. efficient  
   B. efficiently  
   C. efficiency  
   D. more efficient

8. Choose the correct form: The instructions were ____.  
   A. clearly  
   B. clear  
   C. clearness  
   D. clearestly

9. Which sentence means that the speaker has a strong ability to solve problems?  
   A. She works hardly to solve problems.  
   B. She hardly works to solve problems.  
   C. She works hard to solve problems.  
   D. She works lately to solve problems.

10. In *I have not seen the revised schedule lately*, *lately* means:  
    A. not with effort  
    B. recently  
    C. at a late hour  
    D. in a slow manner

11. Which sentence places *only* to show that the speaker reviewed the budget, and no other document?  
    A. Only I reviewed the budget.  
    B. I only reviewed the budget.  
    C. I reviewed only the budget.  
    D. I reviewed the only budget.

12. Which sentence most clearly means the meeting is on Tuesday rather than another day?  
    A. We will meet just on Tuesday.  
    B. Just we will meet on Tuesday.  
    C. We just will meet on Tuesday.  
    D. We will just meet Tuesday on.

13. Which sentence uses an adverb to modify the whole clause?  
    A. Fortunately, the train arrived on time.  
    B. The fortunate team arrived on time.  
    C. The train was fortunate.  
    D. The train arrived at the station.

14. Choose the best form: The applicant spoke ____ during the interview.  
    A. good  
    B. well  
    C. betterly  
    D. bestly

15. Choose the best form: The applicant is a ____ speaker.  
    A. well  
    B. good  
    C. carefully  
    D. badly

16. Which sentence uses *hardly* correctly to mean “almost not”?  
    A. We hardly had time to finish the report.  
    B. We worked hardly all afternoon.  
    C. She studied hardly for the exam.  
    D. They ran hardly to catch the bus.

17. Which sentence uses an adverb of time?  
    A. The package is outside.  
    B. The technician responded yesterday.  
    C. The technician responded politely.  
    D. The technician responded frequently.

18. Which edit removes unnecessary intensifiers and keeps the meaning direct?  
    A. The presentation was very extremely informative.  
    B. The presentation was informative.  
    C. The presentation was really very informative indeed.  
    D. The presentation was informative and very extremely useful.

19. Which sentence means that the speaker does not simply suggest the plan but formally recommends it?  
    A. I merely recommend the plan.  
    B. I recommend merely the plan.  
    C. I recommend the plan merely.  
    D. Merely I recommend the plan.

20. Which word is an adverb modifying another adverb in *The shuttle arrived surprisingly early*?  
    A. shuttle  
    B. arrived  
    C. surprisingly  
    D. early

### Answer key

1. **B — carefully.** It modifies the verb *checked* by describing how the assistant checked.  
2. **C — clear.** *Extremely* is an adverb of degree modifying the adjective *clear*.  
3. **D — confidently.** *Quite* modifies the adverb *confidently*.  
4. **B — rarely.** It tells how often something happens.  
5. **A — nearby.** It indicates place.  
6. **A — weekly.** It tells how often the team meets.  
7. **B — efficiently.** The adverb describes how the employee completed the task.  
8. **B — clear.** The linking verb *were* connects the subject to an adjective describing the instructions.  
9. **C — She works hard to solve problems.** *Hard* means with effort; *hardly* means almost not.  
10. **B — recently.** *Lately* means recently or in the recent period.  
11. **C — I reviewed only the budget.** Here *only* directly precedes the item that was reviewed and excludes other documents.  
12. **A — We will meet just on Tuesday.** *Just* is placed next to the time phrase it limits.  
13. **A — Fortunately, the train arrived on time.** *Fortunately* comments on the whole clause.  
14. **B — well.** It modifies the action *spoke*.  
15. **B — good.** The adjective describes the noun *speaker*.  
16. **A — We hardly had time to finish the report.** *Hardly* means almost no time.  
17. **B — yesterday.** It tells when the technician responded.  
18. **B — The presentation was informative.** Removing stacked intensifiers makes the sentence direct.  
19. **A — I merely recommend the plan.** *Merely* modifies *recommend*, meaning that the speaker recommends but does not take a stronger action such as approve.  
20. **C — surprisingly.** It modifies the adverb *early*.

## Chapter 7 Quiz: Prepositions

### Questions

1. In *The keys are under the folder*, which word is the preposition?  
   A. keys  
   B. are  
   C. under  
   D. folder

2. Which words form the prepositional phrase in *We met after the lecture*?  
   A. We met  
   B. after the lecture  
   C. the lecture  
   D. met after

3. In *She placed the forms on the desk*, what is the object of the preposition *on*?  
   A. She  
   B. placed  
   C. forms  
   D. desk

4. Choose the correct preposition: The webinar begins ____ 3:30 p.m.  
   A. at  
   B. on  
   C. in  
   D. by

5. Choose the correct preposition: The exam is ____ Thursday.  
   A. at  
   B. on  
   C. in  
   D. from

6. Choose the correct preposition: The new term begins ____ September.  
   A. at  
   B. on  
   C. in  
   D. to

7. Choose the correct preposition: The keys are ____ the drawer.  
   A. in  
   B. at  
   C. on  
   D. by

8. Choose the correct preposition: Please leave the application ____ the front desk.  
   A. at  
   B. in  
   C. to  
   D. into

9. Choose the correct preposition: The report is ____ the table.  
   A. at  
   B. on  
   C. in  
   D. from

10. Choose the correct preposition: She walked ____ the room and sat down.  
    A. into  
    B. at  
    C. during  
    D. about

11. Choose the established word partnership: I am interested ____ the internship.  
    A. on  
    B. in  
    C. at  
    D. for

12. Choose the established word partnership: We discussed ____ the budget at the meeting.  
    A. about  
    B. on  
    C. no preposition is needed  
    D. with

13. In *The book on the shelf belongs to Maya*, which prepositional phrase modifies *book*?  
    A. on the shelf  
    B. belongs to Maya  
    C. the book  
    D. to Maya

14. In *They waited at the station*, which phrase gives information about where they waited?  
    A. They waited  
    B. at the station  
    C. the station  
    D. waited at

15. Choose the correct preposition: The package arrived ____ Monday morning.  
    A. at  
    B. on  
    C. in  
    D. to

16. Choose the correct preposition: She has worked here ____ 2022.  
    A. since  
    B. for  
    C. during  
    D. by

17. Choose the correct preposition: The team has been working on the project ____ three weeks.  
    A. since  
    B. for  
    C. during  
    D. at

18. Which sentence avoids an unnecessary preposition?  
    A. We discussed about the schedule.  
    B. We discussed the schedule.  
    C. We discussed on the schedule.  
    D. We discussed over about the schedule.

19. Choose the correct preposition: The meeting will take place ____ the conference room.  
    A. in  
    B. on  
    C. at to  
    D. by into

20. Which sentence uses a prepositional phrase to show direction?  
    A. The students waited quietly.  
    B. The students walked toward the library.  
    C. The students arrived yesterday.  
    D. The students were ready.

### Answer key

1. **C — under.** It shows the relationship between the keys and the folder.  
2. **B — after the lecture.** The phrase begins with the preposition *after* and includes its object, *lecture*, with its determiner.  
3. **D — desk.** *Desk* is the object of *on*.  
4. **A — at.** Use *at* with a specific clock time.  
5. **B — on.** Use *on* with a day of the week.  
6. **C — in.** Use *in* with a month.  
7. **A — in.** *In the drawer* indicates an enclosed location.  
8. **A — at.** *At the front desk* identifies the location where the application should be left.  
9. **B — on.** *On the table* indicates a surface.  
10. **A — into.** *Into* indicates movement from outside to inside.  
11. **B — in.** The established combination is *interested in*.  
12. **C — no preposition is needed.** In this meaning, *discuss* takes a direct object: *discuss the budget*.  
13. **A — on the shelf.** This phrase modifies *book* by identifying which book.  
14. **B — at the station.** The phrase modifies *waited* by giving the location.  
15. **B — on.** Use *on* with a specific day and part of that day: *on Monday morning*.  
16. **A — since.** Use *since* with the starting point of a period.  
17. **B — for.** Use *for* with a duration.  
18. **B — We discussed the schedule.** *Discuss* does not need *about* before its direct object in this meaning.  
19. **A — in.** *In the conference room* indicates location inside a room.  
20. **B — The students walked toward the library.** *Toward the library* shows direction.

## Chapter 8 Quiz: Conjunctions

### Questions

1. Which word is the conjunction in *The draft is clear, but it needs one example*?  
   A. draft  
   B. clear  
   C. but  
   D. example

2. Which coordinating conjunction commonly signals addition?  
   A. and  
   B. but  
   C. or  
   D. yet

3. Choose the conjunction that signals a choice: You can submit the form online ____ bring it to the office.  
   A. and  
   B. but  
   C. or  
   D. so

4. Choose the conjunction that signals a result: The bus was delayed, ____ we arrived late.  
   A. nor  
   B. so  
   C. yet  
   D. for

5. Choose the correct correlative pair: ____ the manager ____ the assistant can approve the request.  
   A. Either / or  
   B. Either / nor  
   C. Both / or  
   D. Neither / and

6. Choose the correct correlative pair: The training is ____ useful ____ practical.  
   A. both / and  
   B. both / or  
   C. neither / and  
   D. either / nor

7. Choose the correct correlative pair: The guide is ____ concise ____ easy to follow.  
   A. not only / but also  
   B. not only / and  
   C. either / but also  
   D. neither / or

8. Which sentence has parallel structure?  
   A. The role involves planning events, answering email, and meeting clients.  
   B. The role involves planning events, to answer email, and meeting clients.  
   C. The role involves planning events, email answers, and meeting clients.  
   D. The role involves to plan events, answering email, and clients.

9. Which word is a subordinating conjunction in *We stayed inside because it was raining*?  
   A. stayed  
   B. inside  
   C. because  
   D. raining

10. Choose the subordinating conjunction that signals contrast: ____ the task was difficult, the team completed it on time.  
    A. Although  
    B. Because  
    C. Unless  
    D. When

11. Choose the subordinating conjunction that signals a condition: ____ you need help, contact the support desk.  
    A. Although  
    B. If  
    C. Since  
    D. Before

12. Which sentence is correctly punctuated?  
    A. I sent the file, and I saved a copy.  
    B. I sent the file and, I saved a copy.  
    C. I sent the file, I saved a copy.  
    D. I sent, the file and I saved a copy.

13. Which sentence correctly joins two independent clauses?  
    A. The room was booked, the team arrived early.  
    B. The room was booked, so the team arrived early.  
    C. The room was booked so, the team arrived early.  
    D. The room was booked, because the team arrived early.

14. Which choice is a sentence fragment?  
    A. Because the printer stopped working.  
    B. The printer stopped working.  
    C. The printer stopped working, so we used another one.  
    D. We used another printer because the first one stopped working.

15. Which sentence is a comma splice?  
    A. The meeting ended, and we returned to work.  
    B. The meeting ended; we returned to work.  
    C. The meeting ended, we returned to work.  
    D. When the meeting ended, we returned to work.

16. Choose the best correction for the comma splice in *The meeting ended, we returned to work.*  
    A. The meeting ended we returned to work.  
    B. The meeting ended, and we returned to work.  
    C. The meeting, ended we returned to work.  
    D. The meeting ended, because we returned to work.

17. Choose the conjunction that signals time: We will send the report ____ the director approves it.  
    A. after  
    B. although  
    C. unless  
    D. because

18. Which sentence correctly uses *neither/nor*?  
    A. Neither the report or the summary is ready.  
    B. Neither the report nor the summary is ready.  
    C. Neither the report and the summary is ready.  
    D. Neither the report but the summary is ready.

19. Choose the best conjunction to show contrast: The schedule is convenient, ____ it is expensive.  
    A. and  
    B. but  
    C. so  
    D. for

20. Which sentence correctly punctuates a dependent clause at the beginning?  
    A. When the review is complete we will send the draft.  
    B. When the review is complete, we will send the draft.  
    C. When, the review is complete we will send the draft.  
    D. When the review, is complete, we will send the draft.

### Answer key

1. **C — but.** It connects two clauses and signals contrast.  
2. **A — and.** It commonly adds a word, phrase, or idea.  
3. **C — or.** It presents alternatives.  
4. **B — so.** It introduces a result.  
5. **A — Either/or.** This pair presents two alternatives.  
6. **A — both/and.** The pair connects two qualities equally.  
7. **A — not only/but also.** These words form the standard correlative pair.  
8. **A.** All three items use *-ing* forms.  
9. **C — because.** It introduces the dependent clause and signals cause.  
10. **A — Although.** It introduces a contrast or concession.  
11. **B — If.** It introduces a condition.  
12. **A.** A comma precedes the coordinating conjunction joining two independent clauses.  
13. **B.** The comma plus *so* correctly joins the independent clauses and indicates a result.  
14. **A.** The *because* clause is dependent and cannot stand alone as a complete sentence.  
15. **C.** It joins two independent clauses with only a comma.  
16. **B.** A comma plus coordinating conjunction correctly joins the independent clauses.  
17. **A — after.** It signals the time relationship between the clauses.  
18. **B.** The standard correlative pair is *neither/nor*.  
19. **B — but.** It signals contrast.  
20. **B.** A comma follows the introductory dependent clause.

## Chapter 9 Quiz: Articles and Determiners

### Questions

1. What is a determiner's basic job?  
   A. To show an action  
   B. To come before a noun and identify or limit it  
   C. To connect two clauses  
   D. To describe how an action happens

2. Which word is an article?  
   A. quickly  
   B. because  
   C. the  
   D. under

3. Choose the correct article: I saw ____ eagle near the river.  
   A. a  
   B. an  
   C. the (always required)  
   D. no article because *eagle* is singular

4. Choose the correct article based on sound: She attends ____ university in the city.  
   A. a  
   B. an  
   C. the (always required)  
   D. no article

5. Choose the correct article based on sound: We waited for ____ hour.  
   A. a  
   B. an  
   C. the (always required)  
   D. no article

6. Which sentence correctly uses first mention and later mention?  
   A. I found the key. A key was under the chair.  
   B. I found a key. The key was under the chair.  
   C. I found key. The a key was under chair.  
   D. I found an key. A key was under the chair.

7. Choose the correct article: Please close ____ door beside you.  
   A. a  
   B. an  
   C. the  
   D. no article

8. Choose the correct form for a general statement: ____ books can introduce readers to new ideas.  
   A. A  
   B. An  
   C. The (always required)  
   D. No article

9. Choose the correct form: We need ____ information about the schedule.  
   A. an  
   B. a  
   C. some  
   D. many

10. Choose the correct form: There are ____ chairs in the meeting room.  
    A. much  
    B. many  
    C. a little  
    D. any of

11. Choose the correct form: We have ____ time before the appointment, so we can review the notes.  
    A. a few  
    B. many  
    C. a little  
    D. several

12. Choose the correct form in a negative sentence: There aren't ____ clean cups left.  
    A. some  
    B. any  
    C. much  
    D. little

13. Choose the correct demonstrative determiner: ____ documents on this desk need signatures. (documents are near the speaker)  
    A. This  
    B. That  
    C. These  
    D. Those

14. Choose the correct possessive determiner: The employees submitted ____ timesheets.  
    A. them  
    B. theirs  
    C. their  
    D. they

15. Choose the correct determiner: ____ applicant must bring identification.  
    A. Every  
    B. Many  
    C. Few  
    D. Several

16. Which sentence uses the zero article for a general noncount noun?  
    A. The information is useful.  
    B. Information is useful.  
    C. An information is useful.  
    D. A information is useful.

17. Choose the natural article in the usual meal expression: We had ____ lunch at noon.  
    A. a  
    B. an  
    C. the  
    D. no article

18. Choose the natural expression for a person who is doing their job: Kai is ____ work until 5 p.m.  
    A. at  
    B. in the  
    C. on a  
    D. to an

19. Choose the correct quantity word: There are ____ errors in this version than in the first one.  
    A. less  
    B. fewer  
    C. little  
    D. much

20. Which sentence correctly uses an indefinite article with a singular count noun?  
    A. She submitted report yesterday.  
    B. She submitted a report yesterday.  
    C. She submitted an report yesterday.  
    D. She submitted reports yesterday a.

### Answer key

1. **B.** A determiner comes before a noun and identifies or limits it.  
2. **C — the.** *The* is the definite article.  
3. **B — an.** *Eagle* begins with a vowel sound.  
4. **A — a.** *University* begins with a consonant sound (/y/), even though it begins with the letter *u*.  
5. **B — an.** The *h* in *hour* is silent, so the word begins with a vowel sound.  
6. **B.** *A key* introduces a nonspecific key; *the key* refers back to that identified key.  
7. **C — the.** The phrase *beside you* identifies a particular door in the shared context.  
8. **D — no article.** A plural count noun used generally does not need an article.  
9. **C — some.** *Information* is noncount; *some information* is correct.  
10. **B — many.** *Chairs* is a plural count noun.  
11. **C — a little.** *Time* is noncount, and *a little* means a small amount; the context shows that amount is sufficient.  
12. **B — any.** *Any* is common in negative statements with plural count nouns.  
13. **C — These.** It matches the plural noun *documents* and indicates nearby items.  
14. **C — their.** A possessive determiner comes before the noun *timesheets*.  
15. **A — Every.** *Every* is followed by a singular count noun and refers to members individually.  
16. **B — Information is useful.** A general noncount noun often takes no article.  
17. **D — no article.** The usual expression is *have lunch*.  
18. **A — at.** *At work* is the natural expression for being at one's workplace or doing one's job.  
19. **B — fewer.** *Errors* is a plural count noun.  
20. **B — She submitted a report yesterday.** A singular count noun needs a determiner, and *a* precedes the consonant sound in *report*.

## Scoring and review

Count one point for each correct response in a chapter quiz. Use the result to identify topics to revisit, not to assign yourself a proficiency level. Review missed questions in the matching chapter and then write an original sentence using the same pattern. If more than one answer seems possible, check the context stated in the prompt and the explanation in the key.
