# Chapter Quizzes: Chapters 10–13

Each chapter quiz contains 20 items. Choose the best answer unless the prompt says otherwise. Answer keys explain the grammatical choice or the communication decision being tested.

---

## Chapter 10 Quiz: Interjections

### Questions

1. Which word is the interjection in *Wow, that was a clear presentation*?  
   A. that  
   B. was  
   C. clear  
   D. Wow

2. What is the main job of an interjection?  
   A. To name a person or place  
   B. To convey a reaction or manage interaction  
   C. To connect two independent clauses  
   D. To show possession

3. Which interjection most naturally signals that the speaker has understood something?  
   A. Oh  
   B. Hey  
   C. Ouch  
   D. Hurray

4. Which interjection is most likely to sound like a greeting or an attempt to get someone's attention?  
   A. Alas  
   B. Hey  
   C. Oops  
   D. Wow

5. Which sentence uses punctuation to show a brief, mild reaction?  
   A. Oh, I see the updated schedule.  
   B. Oh I see the updated schedule?  
   C. Oh; I see the updated schedule!  
   D. Oh: I see the updated schedule.

6. Which version conveys stronger surprise?  
   A. Wow, the project is complete.  
   B. Wow! The project is complete.  
   C. The project, wow, is complete?  
   D. Wow; the project is complete.

7. Which interjection best fits a casual message reacting to a small mistake?  
   A. Oops  
   B. Therefore  
   C. Because  
   D. Whoever

8. Which sentence is most appropriate for a formal report?  
   A. Wow, the number of responses increased.  
   B. The number of responses increased by 12 percent.  
   C. Hey, the number of responses increased.  
   D. Oops, the number of responses increased.

9. What does *well* do in *Well, we have two options*?  
   A. It names the options.  
   B. It introduces the speaker's response or transition.  
   C. It modifies the noun *options*.  
   D. It connects two independent clauses with a conjunction.

10. Which sentence uses an interjection in dialogue?  
    A. “Oh, I left my notebook in the classroom,” Mina said.  
    B. Mina oh left my notebook in the classroom said.  
    C. Mina left, oh my notebook in the classroom said.  
    D. “Mina left my notebook oh in the classroom,” said.

11. Which interjection best expresses pain after bumping a knee?  
    A. Ouch  
    B. Well  
    C. Hey  
    D. Ah, in a formal citation

12. In which setting are interjections generally most at home?  
    A. An informal conversation  
    B. A formal research abstract  
    C. A contract clause  
    D. A technical data table

13. Which message best fits a professional email to a colleague about a minor scheduling error?  
    A. Oops! I booked the room for 3 p.m. instead of 2 p.m. I have corrected it.  
    B. Oops!!! I totally messed everything up!!!  
    C. Wow, hey, the room is wrong, I guess.  
    D. Hurray! I booked the wrong room.

14. What is one effect of placing an interjection at the beginning of a sentence?  
    A. It can frame the speaker's reaction before the main statement.  
    B. It always changes the sentence into a question.  
    C. It makes the following noun plural.  
    D. It removes the need for a subject and verb.

15. Which punctuation mark most often separates a mild opening interjection from the rest of the sentence?  
    A. Comma  
    B. Apostrophe  
    C. Hyphen  
    D. Parenthesis

16. Which sentence uses *alas* in a way that signals disappointment or regret?  
    A. Alas, the final train has already left.  
    B. Alas, please send the invoice.  
    C. Alas, the notebook is on the desk.  
    D. Alas, I will meet you at 2 p.m. (neutral schedule)

17. Which statement about interjections is accurate?  
    A. Every interjection must be followed by an exclamation point.  
    B. Interjections can be punctuated differently depending on their force and placement.  
    C. Interjections can appear only in dialogue.  
    D. Interjections are always inappropriate in writing.

18. Which sentence is likely to sound less formal because of its interjection?  
    A. The equipment arrived on Tuesday.  
    B. Hey, the equipment arrived on Tuesday.  
    C. The equipment arrived on Tuesday, according to the receipt.  
    D. The receipt confirms that the equipment arrived on Tuesday.

19. Which revision is most suitable for a neutral academic sentence?  
    A. Wow, the study found a difference between the groups.  
    B. The study found a difference between the groups.  
    C. Hurray, the study found a difference between the groups.  
    D. Hey, the study found a difference between the groups.

20. In *Oh! I did not see the notice*, why is an exclamation point used after *Oh*?  
    A. To mark a stronger or more emphatic reaction  
    B. To show that *Oh* is a possessive noun  
    C. To join two independent clauses  
    D. To indicate a plural

### Answer key

1. **D — Wow.** It expresses a reaction.  
2. **B.** Interjections express reactions or help manage an interaction.  
3. **A — Oh.** It can signal recognition or understanding.  
4. **B — Hey.** It commonly gets attention or serves as an informal greeting.  
5. **A.** A comma separates the mild interjection from the statement.  
6. **B.** An exclamation point gives *Wow* greater force.  
7. **A — Oops.** It commonly signals a small mistake.  
8. **B.** It states the finding directly and neutrally without a conversational interjection.  
9. **B.** *Well* frames the speaker's response and introduces the next thought.  
10. **A.** The interjection appears naturally in dialogue and is punctuated clearly.  
11. **A — Ouch.** It is a conventional expression of pain.  
12. **A.** Interjections are common in conversation and informal communication.  
13. **A.** It is conversational but professional: the issue and correction are stated clearly.  
14. **A.** A sentence-initial interjection can show the speaker's reaction before the main point.  
15. **A — Comma.** A comma commonly follows a mild introductory interjection.  
16. **A.** *Alas* expresses regret about the missed train.  
17. **B.** A comma or exclamation point may be appropriate depending on emphasis and placement.  
18. **B.** *Hey* gives the statement a more conversational tone.  
19. **B.** It presents the finding in a neutral academic style.  
20. **A.** The exclamation point marks a stronger reaction than a comma would.

## Chapter 11 Quiz: Agreement

### Questions

1. Choose the correct verb: The list of required forms ____ on the website.  
   A. are  
   B. is  
   C. have  
   D. were

2. Choose the correct verb: The forms on the website ____ updated weekly.  
   A. is  
   B. was  
   C. are  
   D. has

3. Choose the correct verb: My colleague and supervisor ____ attending the meeting. (The sentence refers to two different people.)  
   A. is  
   B. are  
   C. has  
   D. was

4. Choose the correct verb: Either the assistant or the managers ____ the room key.  
   A. has  
   B. have  
   C. is  
   D. was

5. Choose the correct verb: Each of the participants ____ a name tag.  
   A. receive  
   B. receives  
   C. are receiving  
   D. have received

6. Choose the correct verb: Several of the participants ____ a name tag.  
   A. receives  
   B. has received  
   C. receive  
   D. is receiving

7. Choose the correct verb for American standard edited usage when the committee is acting as one unit: The committee ____ its recommendation tomorrow.  
   A. announce  
   B. announces  
   C. have announced  
   D. were announcing

8. Choose the correct verb: ____ there enough chairs for everyone?  
   A. Is  
   B. Are  
   C. Has  
   D. Does

9. Choose the correct verb: There ____ two open positions in the department.  
   A. is  
   B. was  
   C. are  
   D. has

10. Choose the correct verb: A number of students ____ signed up for the workshop.  
    A. has  
    B. have  
    C. is  
    D. was

11. Choose the correct verb: The number of students in the workshop ____ increased.  
    A. have  
    B. are  
    C. has  
    D. were

12. Choose the correct verb: The news ____ encouraging.  
    A. are  
    B. were  
    C. is  
    D. have

13. Choose the correct pronoun: The laptop lost ____ connection during the call.  
    A. their  
    B. its  
    C. it's  
    D. them

14. Choose the correct pronoun: Every employee should update ____ emergency contact information.  
    A. their  
    B. them  
    C. theirs  
    D. themselves

15. Which sentence uses a clear singular *they* reference?  
    A. Alex called Morgan after they finished the interview.  
    B. Alex uses they/them pronouns. They will lead the interview today.  
    C. The team told the manager they were ready, but it was unclear who was ready.  
    D. The report and the email were revised before they was sent.

16. Choose the correct pronoun: The two reports are complete; ____ are ready to submit.  
    A. it  
    B. they  
    C. he  
    D. she

17. Choose the correct verb in this question: ____ the delivery arrive this morning?  
    A. Do  
    B. Does  
    C. Are  
    D. Have

18. Choose the correct verb: Where ____ the copies of the contract?  
    A. is  
    B. was  
    C. are  
    D. has

19. Choose the verb that agrees with the subject: The quality of the materials ____ important.  
    A. are  
    B. is  
    C. have  
    D. were

20. Choose the correct verb: Ten dollars ____ enough for the registration fee. (Treat the amount as one total.)  
    A. are  
    B. were  
    C. is  
    D. have

### Answer key

1. **B — is.** The subject is singular *list*; *of required forms* is an intervening phrase.  
2. **C — are.** The subject *forms* is plural.  
3. **B — are.** Two different people joined by *and* form a plural subject.  
4. **B — have.** With *either/or*, the verb commonly agrees with the nearer subject, *managers*.  
5. **B — receives.** *Each* is singular in this construction.  
6. **C — receive.** *Several* refers to multiple participants and takes a plural verb.  
7. **B — announces.** In the stated American usage, the collective noun *committee* is treated as one unit.  
8. **B — Are.** The following noun phrase *enough chairs* is plural, so use *are there*.  
9. **C — are.** The following noun phrase *two open positions* is plural.  
10. **B — have.** *A number of* means “several” and takes a plural verb.  
11. **C — has.** *The number* is the singular subject.  
12. **C — is.** *News* ends in *s* but is normally singular.  
13. **B — its.** The possessive determiner refers to *laptop*.  
14. **A — their.** Singular *they/their* is standard when the person's gender is unknown or irrelevant.  
15. **B.** The sentence makes the antecedent and the person's pronouns clear.  
16. **B — they.** The antecedent *two reports* is plural.  
17. **B — Does.** The subject *delivery* is singular; *does* is followed by the base verb *arrive*.  
18. **C — are.** The subject *copies* is plural; the sentence has inverted word order.  
19. **B — is.** The subject is singular *quality*, not *materials*.  
20. **C — is.** The amount is treated as one total.

## Chapter 12 Quiz: Phrases and Clauses

### Questions

1. Which definition best describes a phrase?  
   A. A group of words that always contains a subject and a finite verb  
   B. A group of related words that functions as a unit but lacks a complete subject-verb combination  
   C. A complete sentence with end punctuation  
   D. A word that joins two independent clauses

2. Which group of words is a clause?  
   A. under the old bridge  
   B. after the lecture  
   C. because the bus was late  
   D. the blue folder

3. Which is a prepositional phrase?  
   A. the student finished  
   B. near the entrance  
   C. is writing  
   D. they called

4. Which is a verb phrase?  
   A. has been working  
   B. at the office  
   C. the new manager  
   D. because of the delay

5. Which group is an independent clause?  
   A. although the room was quiet  
   B. when the class ended  
   C. the students reviewed their notes  
   D. because of the schedule

6. Which group is a dependent clause?  
   A. We submitted the form.  
   B. The bus arrived.  
   C. If the office is open  
   D. Students need breaks.

7. In *What the instructor explained was helpful*, which group is the noun clause?  
   A. What the instructor explained  
   B. the instructor  
   C. was helpful  
   D. helpful

8. In *The student who asked the question stayed afterward*, which clause modifies *student*?  
   A. The student  
   B. who asked the question  
   C. stayed afterward  
   D. the question

9. In *We stayed inside because it was raining*, which clause explains why we stayed inside?  
   A. We stayed inside  
   B. because it was raining  
   C. it was raining  
   D. inside because

10. Which sentence contains a restrictive relative clause, with no commas needed around the clause?  
    A. My colleague, who works in finance, will lead the meeting.  
    B. Students who submit the form by Friday will receive confirmation.  
    C. The report, which is on the desk, needs a signature.  
    D. Our building, which opened in 1998, has a new entrance.

11. Which sentence correctly punctuates a nonrestrictive relative clause?  
    A. My laptop which I bought last year needs a new battery.  
    B. My laptop, which I bought last year, needs a new battery.  
    C. My laptop which, I bought last year needs a new battery.  
    D. My laptop, which I bought last year needs a new battery.

12. Which group of words is a noun phrase?  
    A. the two new interns  
    B. very carefully  
    C. across the hallway  
    D. arrived early

13. Which group is an adverb phrase modifying a verb?  
    A. extremely useful  
    B. with great care  
    C. the front office  
    D. the person in charge

14. Which sentence is a fragment because it is a dependent clause standing alone?  
    A. Although the deadline was extended.  
    B. The deadline was extended.  
    C. The deadline was extended, so the team had another day.  
    D. The team had another day because the deadline was extended.

15. Which sentence has a misplaced modifier?  
    A. She almost drove her children to school every day.  
    B. She drove her children to school almost every day.  
    C. She drove her children to school every weekday.  
    D. She drove her children to school before work.

16. Which sentence has a dangling modifier?  
    A. After reviewing the budget, the director approved the plan.  
    B. After reviewing the budget, the plan was approved by the director.  
    C. The director reviewed the budget and approved the plan.  
    D. The plan was approved after the director reviewed the budget.

17. Which revision repairs the dangling modifier in *Walking to class, the rain began*?  
    A. Walking to class, the rain began suddenly.  
    B. While I was walking to class, it began to rain.  
    C. The rain walking to class began.  
    D. Walking to class, there was rain.

18. Which sentence contains a dependent adverb clause of time?  
    A. We left after the meeting ended.  
    B. We left after the meeting.  
    C. The meeting ended at noon.  
    D. The meeting after lunch ended.

19. Which word introduces a relative clause in *The file that you requested is attached*?  
    A. file  
    B. that  
    C. requested  
    D. attached

20. Which sentence expands the simple idea *The researcher presented the findings* with a clear introductory dependent clause?  
    A. After the data were analyzed, the researcher presented the findings.  
    B. After analyzing the data. The researcher presented the findings.  
    C. The researcher after the data were analyzed presented.  
    D. The researcher presented the findings after. 

### Answer key

1. **B.** A phrase functions as a unit but does not contain a complete subject-verb combination.  
2. **C — because the bus was late.** It contains a subject and verb, so it is a dependent clause.  
3. **B — near the entrance.** It begins with preposition *near* and includes its object.  
4. **A — has been working.** It is a verb phrase with helping verbs and a main verb.  
5. **C — the students reviewed their notes.** It has a subject and verb and can stand as a complete thought.  
6. **C — If the office is open.** The subordinating word *if* makes the clause dependent.  
7. **A — What the instructor explained.** The clause functions as the subject of *was helpful*.  
8. **B — who asked the question.** The relative clause describes *student*.  
9. **B — because it was raining.** It is an adverb clause of cause.  
10. **B.** The clause identifies which students; it is essential to the meaning and is not set off with commas.  
11. **B.** The clause adds nonessential information and is enclosed by commas.  
12. **A — the two new interns.** It is a noun phrase centered on the noun *interns*.  
13. **B — with great care.** The phrase modifies an action by explaining how it is done.  
14. **A.** The *although* clause is dependent and cannot stand alone.  
15. **A.** *Almost* is placed so it can suggest that she nearly drove, rather than that she drove on most days.  
16. **B.** Grammatically, *the plan* appears to be doing the reviewing. Revise to name the director as the reviewer.  
17. **B.** It identifies *I* as the person walking and makes the rain the event that began.  
18. **A.** *After the meeting ended* has a subject and verb and tells when we left.  
19. **B — that.** It introduces the relative clause *that you requested*.  
20. **A.** The introductory dependent clause is complete and is followed by the independent clause.

## Chapter 13 Quiz: Sentences

### Questions

1. Which group is a complete sentence?  
   A. While the class was meeting.  
   B. After finishing the report.  
   C. The class met online.  
   D. Because of the delay.

2. In *The editor reviewed the article*, what is the subject?  
   A. editor  
   B. reviewed  
   C. article  
   D. the

3. In *The editor reviewed the article*, what is the predicate?  
   A. The editor  
   B. reviewed the article  
   C. the article  
   D. reviewed

4. In *The students elected Maya president*, what is the direct object?  
   A. students  
   B. elected  
   C. Maya  
   D. president

5. In *The room became quiet*, what is *quiet*?  
   A. subject  
   B. direct object  
   C. subject complement  
   D. conjunction

6. What is the purpose of this sentence? *Please submit the form by Friday.*  
   A. Declarative  
   B. Interrogative  
   C. Imperative  
   D. Exclamatory

7. What is the purpose of this sentence? *Where is the nearest bus stop?*  
   A. Declarative  
   B. Interrogative  
   C. Imperative  
   D. Exclamatory

8. Which is a simple sentence?  
   A. The office closes at five.  
   B. The office closes at five, and the staff leave.  
   C. When the office closes, the staff leave.  
   D. When the office closes, the staff leave, and the lights turn off.

9. Which is a compound sentence?  
   A. The report is complete.  
   B. The report is complete, and the editor approved it.  
   C. When the report is complete, send it to the editor.  
   D. The report that the editor approved is complete.

10. Which is a complex sentence?  
    A. The bus arrived, and we boarded.  
    B. The bus arrived late.  
    C. Because the bus arrived late, we missed the opening remarks.  
    D. The bus arrived late, and we missed the opening remarks, but we joined the discussion.

11. Which is a compound-complex sentence?  
    A. The team met after lunch.  
    B. The team met, and the manager took notes.  
    C. After the team met, the manager took notes, and the assistant sent a summary.  
    D. After the team met, the manager took notes.

12. Which sentence correctly joins two independent clauses?  
    A. The printer stopped, we called support.  
    B. The printer stopped; we called support.  
    C. The printer stopped, because we called support.  
    D. The printer stopped we called support.

13. Which sentence contains a comma splice?  
    A. The room was full, so we opened another one.  
    B. The room was full; we opened another one.  
    C. The room was full, we opened another one.  
    D. Because the room was full, we opened another one.

14. Which is a sentence fragment?  
    A. The office reopened on Monday.  
    B. Although the office reopened on Monday.  
    C. The office reopened on Monday, and staff returned.  
    D. Staff returned when the office reopened on Monday.

15. Choose the best correction for the run-on: *The event ended we cleaned the room.*  
    A. The event ended, we cleaned the room.  
    B. The event ended; we cleaned the room.  
    C. The event, ended we cleaned the room.  
    D. The event ended because, we cleaned the room.

16. Choose the best correction for the fragment: *Because the file was missing.*  
    A. Because the file was missing, we requested another copy.  
    B. Because the file was missing. We requested another copy.  
    C. The file because was missing another copy.  
    D. Because the file missing another copy.

17. Which revision uses subordination to show that one idea is the reason for another?  
    A. The road was closed, so we took another route.  
    B. Because the road was closed, we took another route.  
    C. The road was closed; we took another route.  
    D. The road was closed. Another route.

18. Which sentence is the most concise while preserving the meaning?  
    A. We made a decision to postpone the meeting.  
    B. We decided to postpone the meeting.  
    C. We made a decision that was about postponing the meeting.  
    D. We did the act of deciding to postpone the meeting.

19. Which sentence puts the most emphasis on the result rather than the person who performed the action?  
    A. The technician repaired the equipment.  
    B. The equipment was repaired before the inspection.  
    C. The technician, who repaired the equipment, arrived early.  
    D. The technician repaired the equipment, and then left.

20. Which revision has sentence variety while keeping the relationship clear?  
    A. The team checked the data. The team found an error. The team corrected it.  
    B. After checking the data, the team found an error and corrected it.  
    C. Checking the data. The team found an error and corrected it.  
    D. The data checked the team, found an error, and corrected it.

### Answer key

1. **C — The class met online.** It is an independent clause with a complete thought.  
2. **A — editor.** The editor performs the action.  
3. **B — reviewed the article.** The predicate tells what the subject did.  
4. **C — Maya.** She receives the action of *elected*.  
5. **C — subject complement.** *Quiet* describes the subject after linking verb *became*.  
6. **C — Imperative.** It gives a direction or request; the understood subject is *you*.  
7. **B — Interrogative.** It asks a question.  
8. **A.** It contains one independent clause.  
9. **B.** It contains two independent clauses joined with comma + *and*.  
10. **C.** It has one independent clause and a dependent clause beginning with *because*.  
11. **C.** It contains the dependent clause *After the team met* and two independent clauses joined with *and*.  
12. **B.** A semicolon can join closely related independent clauses.  
13. **C.** It joins two independent clauses with only a comma.  
14. **B.** The *although* clause is dependent and cannot stand alone.  
15. **B.** A semicolon correctly joins the two independent clauses. A period or comma plus a suitable conjunction would also be grammatical repairs, but B is the listed correct choice.  
16. **A.** It attaches the dependent *because* clause to an independent clause.  
17. **B.** The dependent clause introduced by *because* explains the reason for taking another route.  
18. **B.** *Decided* expresses the meaning directly with fewer words.  
19. **B.** The passive sentence foregrounds the equipment and the result; the doer is not named.  
20. **B.** The introductory phrase connects the checking to the discovery and correction without repeating the subject.

## Scoring and review

Count one point for each correct answer in a chapter quiz. Use your results to choose what to review, not to assign a proficiency label. Revisit missed items in the relevant chapter and try making a new example. Where a sentence can be revised in more than one way, compare the answer key's explanation with the meaning and audience you intend.
